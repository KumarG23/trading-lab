# Successful Trader and AI/Systematic Trading Research — 2026-08-18

## Decision

The Trading Lab should not imitate a famous trader's visible entry rule. The durable commonality among successful traders and systematic firms is a research-and-risk operating system:

1. trade only a demonstrable edge;
2. size by risk and opportunity quality rather than conviction prose;
3. use multiple genuinely different signals, markets, and horizons;
4. include costs, liquidity, fills, capacity, and concentration in the objective;
5. preserve failed experiments and untouched forward evidence;
6. adapt through controlled champion/challenger tests, never by tuning yesterday's loss away.

The current V1 suite remains useful as a frozen research control, but it has not earned portfolio admission. Its execution-shaped lane is disabled by default while research capture continues. The first V2 change is point-in-time scanner-context capture so catalyst/stocks-in-play hypotheses can be evaluated prospectively without reconstructing hindsight features.

## Evidence ladder

1. Independently audited live net results or strong institutional due diligence.
2. Live manager-reported results with substantial external capital.
3. Peer-reviewed out-of-sample research with realistic costs and replication material.
4. Working papers or backtests with incomplete execution evidence.
5. Marketing, social posts, anecdotes, and unverified return screenshots.

A lower-tier source may create a hypothesis. It cannot justify promotion, capital, or weaker gates.

## Who has been unusually successful, and what is actually transferable?

### Jim Simons / Renaissance Technologies — systematic statistical ensembles

Reported evidence: Institutional Investor reports Medallion at roughly 39% annualized net from 1988–2021, with thousands of short-term long and short positions across markets. Exact signals, leverage, data, execution, and capacity are private.

What appears durable:

- scientific teams rather than one market oracle;
- many small statistical effects combined into an ensemble;
- long and short exposure across instruments;
- relentless data cleaning, model testing, and execution engineering;
- rapid retirement or modification of decaying effects;
- scale and diversification across many bets rather than one setup family.

Transferability: the research process is highly transferable; the actual edge, leverage, latency, data estate, and market-impact infrastructure are not. “Use AI like Renaissance” is not a strategy specification.

Sources:

- https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2e0uykr3vn5booz0smrcw/hedge-funds/renaissances-2024-rebirth
- https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswymr8cih3jeaslxc00/portfolio/famed-medallion-fund-stretches-explanation-to-the-limit-professor-claims

### Stanley Druckenmiller — asymmetry, concentration, and fast thesis invalidation

Reported evidence: Morgan Stanley describes Duquesne's approximately 30% annualized record with no losing years from 1981–2010. Druckenmiller emphasizes that sizing determines how much is made when right and lost when wrong.

What appears durable:

- wait for unusually favorable setups instead of constant activity;
- express a coherent macro/regime thesis across the most suitable instrument;
- concentrate only after evidence and timing align;
- monitor the thesis continuously and exit quickly when facts change;
- judge success by payoff asymmetry, not win rate.

Transferability: explicit no-trade states, regime evidence, and payoff-aware sizing transfer. Concentrated leveraged macro bets do not belong in a $200 intraday lab.

Sources:

- https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/videos/hard-lessons/duquesne-stan-druckenmiller-iliana-bouzali
- https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/talks-at-gs/stanley-druckenmiller

### Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway — quality, price, patience, and compounding

Buffett is an investor rather than a day trader, but his record provides the cleanest warning against unnecessary turnover.

What appears durable:

- demand understandable economics and a margin of safety;
- make few decisions when evidence is weak;
- avoid friction and allow favorable economics to compound;
- distinguish business quality from a temporarily attractive price pattern.

Transferability: abstention, evidence quality, and cost aversion transfer. Multi-year fundamental compounding does not map directly to one-minute intraday signals.

Sources:

- https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html
- https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html

### Systematic trend followers / David Harding and Winton — simple mechanism, broad diversification

Winton's official history describes its foundation in systematic trend-following futures and subsequent expansion through research and diversification. AQR's research documents time-series momentum across equities, fixed income, commodities, and currencies over a century-scale sample.

What appears durable:

- define trends mechanically;
- trade both directions;
- volatility-scale risk;
- diversify across independent markets and horizons;
- tolerate many small losses so infrequent persistent trends can pay;
- keep rules stable enough to measure.

Transferability: multi-horizon trend state, two-sided candidates, volatility scaling, and cross-market/session diversification transfer. Futures leverage and century-scale holding periods do not map directly to tiny-account one-minute equities.

Sources:

- https://www.winton.com/history
- https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing
- https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum

### Paul Tudor Jones and other discretionary macro traders — defense first

Public interviews repeatedly attribute importance to trend state, predefined exits, avoiding averaging down, and seeking favorable reward/risk. Public evidence about exact rules is less rigorous than the sources above.

Transferability: hard invalidation, no adding to losers, and regime-aware abstention are already compatible with the lab. A 200-day moving average is a risk/regime feature, not proof of an intraday edge.

## Credible AI/ML trading success

### Man AHL — ML inside a mature systematic stack

Evidence tier: 2. Man AHL reports live ML systems in multi-strategy client portfolios since 2014. Public materials emphasize structured data, NLP, execution, reinforcement learning for routing, transaction costs, and diversified systematic portfolios.

What they do differently:

- ML is not the risk officer or an unconstrained trade narrator;
- research, prediction, portfolio construction, and execution are separate layers;
- transaction costs and route-to-market quality are first-class;
- multiple strategies and horizons reduce dependence on one fragile setup.

Sources:

- https://www.man.com/insights/the-rise-of-machine-learning
- https://www.man.com/insights/intro-machine-learning
- https://www.man.com/insights/the-big-picture-ai

### Numerai — crowd ensemble and exposure neutralization

Evidence tier: 2. Numerai reports a 25.45% net 2024 return and 2.75 Sharpe; JPMorgan secured up to $500 million in capacity. Industry reporting also records a substantial 2023 loss, which matters: this is a live but non-magical process.

What they do differently:

- many independent models contribute predictions;
- contribution and low correlation matter more than standalone accuracy;
- portfolio construction neutralizes unwanted exposures;
- model submission, evaluation, and capital allocation are separate.

Transferable mechanism: measure incremental out-of-sample utility and correlation before ensembling. Four strategies reacting to the same ticker/minute are not four independent signals.

Sources:

- https://blog.numer.ai/jpmorgan-secures-500m-capacity/
- https://www.hedgeweek.com/quant-hedge-fund-numerai-secures-500m-jpmorgan-allocation/

### Voleon — institutional machine-learning statistical arbitrage

Evidence tier: 2, with important limits. Voleon publicly identifies machine learning and statistical modeling as its core process; institutional press reports double-digit recent gains and pension interest. Exact net history, models, turnover, leverage, costs, and execution are not public enough to reproduce.

Transferable mechanism: cross-sectional prediction, many positions, neutrality, and institutional data/engineering. Not transferable: pretending a local LLM reviewing four hand-written indicators is equivalent.

Sources:

- https://voleon.com/
- https://www.pionline.com/alternatives/machine-learning-quant-firm-voleon-posts-double-digit-gains-pension-funds-are-taking/

### Two Sigma — institutional ML plus a governance warning

Evidence tier: 2 for institutional deployment, not public strategy-level performance attribution. Two Sigma's SEC registration establishes a large quantitative adviser, but the more useful public lesson is negative: a 2025 SEC action described failures around unapproved model changes and related controls.

Transferable mechanism: model versioning, independent review, immutable trial records, deployment authorization, and monitoring are part of the trading strategy—not paperwork added afterward. An apparently better model must not silently replace the champion.

Sources:

- https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/firm/summary/137137
- https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15
- https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2025/34-102207.pdf

### Citadel Securities and Virtu — execution businesses, not retail signal recipes

Audited filings establish large, technology-driven market-making businesses. They do not establish that a retail user can copy their edge from chart indicators or an LLM. Their economics depend on continuous two-sided quoting, exchange connectivity, order-flow relationships, inventory/risk netting, capital, and execution infrastructure.

Transferable mechanism: latency measurement, fill probability, market impact, inventory/exposure controls, reconciliation, and kill switches. Not transferable: market-making economics without market-making access.

Sources:

- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1146184/000114618426000004/CDRG_BS_Only_FS_2025.pdf
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000159238626000009/virt-20251231.htm

### Peer-reviewed financial ML

Gu, Kelly, and Xiu show that nonlinear tabular models can add out-of-sample economic value in cross-sectional asset pricing. The correct lesson is to compare transparent linear models with trees/nonlinear models on structured point-in-time data—not to place trades from prose.

Jensen, Kelly, Malamud, and Pedersen show why prediction and implementation must be joined through costs, turnover, and constraints. Cost-agnostic ML often selects fleeting signals that are not tradable.

Sources:

- https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398
- https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2223/5758276
- https://academic.oup.com/rfs/advance-article/doi/10.1093/rfs/hhag022/8524346

## What successful systems do differently from Trading Lab V1

| Dimension | Successful systematic pattern | V1 condition | Required correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edge | Many validated weak effects or rare high-conviction setups | Four related long-only technical setups | Add genuinely different, registered hypotheses; keep V1 frozen |
| Direction | Long and short where mandate permits | Long-only | Research short candidates separately before any execution use |
| Opportunity set | Many markets, assets, or cross-sectional choices | Approximately 30 equities/ETFs from one scanner | Preserve exact point-in-time scanner context and diversify only when evidence supports it |
| Data | Point-in-time, cleaned, often alternative/catalyst data | Mostly one-minute OHLCV | Capture timestamped scanner/catalyst features prospectively |
| Objective | Net utility after costs, fills, turnover, capacity | Costs modeled, but raw strategy edge remains negative | Keep cost-aware ranking deferred until gross edge exists |
| Portfolio | Correlation/exposure-aware | Two-slot admission over correlated candidates | Freeze portfolio admission; later use top-k with cluster/exposure controls |
| AI role | Structured prediction, execution, research automation | Local LLM shadow annotations | Keep LLM outside authority; tabular models remain the prediction lane |
| Evolution | Champion/challenger, trial registry, retirement rules | Weekly audit exists; V1 remained operational despite failed gates | Enforce baseline freeze and one active challenger at a time |

## Implemented V2 foundation

### 1. Freeze portfolio admission, continue research

`TRADING_LAB_PORTFOLIO_ADMISSION_ENABLED` now defaults to false. New candidates remain logged and receive realistic research-lifecycle outcomes, but they cannot be marked portfolio-admitted unless the setting is explicitly enabled later. This prevents the negative V1 suite from continuing to masquerade as an execution-shaped portfolio while retaining its value as a control.

This does not enable broker orders. The lab remains `paper_proposal_only_no_orders`.

### 2. Capture point-in-time scanner context

The proposal loop now accepts scanner metadata only when:

- both the snapshot creation time and completed-bar data cutoff are timezone-aware;
- both timestamps are from the same Eastern trading session as the decision;
- neither timestamp is later than the decision;
- the data cutoff is not later than snapshot creation;
- a recomputed SHA-256 of the cutoff, ranked rows, and watchlist matches the artifact;
- fields are namespaced with `scanner_`.

Captured fields include scanner score/rank, move, relative volume, dollar volume, range, price, snapshot timestamp, completed-bar cutoff, and content hash. The producer floors its request to the current minute boundary and discards bars at or after that boundary, preventing an in-progress minute from entering the score. `scanner_score`, already present in the v5 feature contract, can now become populated in forward evidence without modifying immutable historical data. Other fields remain provenance for a separately versioned future corpus.

Scanner provenance is retained on every generated strategy candidate so the immutable control ledger has a consistent point-in-time annotation. The preregistered primary analysis cohort for `ATL-H-2026-08-18-01` is ORB and momentum only; VWAP cohorts are exploratory diagnostics and cannot satisfy this challenger's promotion gate.

This is intentionally data capture, not a favorable filter. Filtering immediately would select on an unvalidated feature and contaminate the forward comparison.

## Champion/challenger evolution loop

### Champion

- Frozen V1 research suite: ORB, VWAP trend/imbalance, VWAP reclaim, momentum pullback.
- Purpose: unchanged control and data-quality sentinel.
- Portfolio admission: disabled.

### Active challenger

**ATL-H-2026-08-18-01 — point-in-time stocks-in-play context for ORB/momentum**

- Mechanism: test whether pre-decision scanner strength, abnormal volume, move, and range identify the small subset where breakout/trend behavior survives costs.
- Status: forward data collection only.
- Minimum evidence before selection: 100 independent forward observations across at least 20 sessions.
- Development gates: positive net expectancy, profit factor >1.2, positive session-level expectancy, no single symbol/strategy/regime concentration, and robustness under double-slippage stress.
- Holdout rule: score a later untouched forward period once, only after development gates pass.
- Failure: reject or redesign if development expectancy remains non-positive, gains vanish after costs, or performance is concentrated.

### Queued challengers — not yet implemented

1. **Two-sided multi-horizon trend state**
   - Add separately versioned short candidates and slower trend context.
   - Do not mirror long rules blindly; borrow availability and short-sale constraints must be modeled.

2. **Catalyst-timestamp features**
   - Earnings/news timing, premarket gap, abnormal premarket volume.
   - Requires a durable point-in-time source with timestamps and replay rights.

3. **Cross-sectional net-utility ranking**
   - Rank contemporaneous candidates by expected net R and select constrained top-k.
   - Deferred until at least one component shows positive gross/net development evidence.

4. **Diverse horizon expansion**
   - Test hourly/daily swing or ETF trend hypotheses separately from the one-minute suite.
   - A different horizon may be more valuable than another one-minute indicator.

## Weekly operating contract

The existing Saturday evidence steward is the correct backbone. Its cycle should remain:

1. lock and hash the corpus/manifest;
2. verify point-in-time availability and no-order state;
3. evaluate champion and one active challenger on purged chronology;
4. aggregate by independent session, not raw candidate count;
5. run cost, fill, concentration, regime, and drift diagnostics;
6. register every attempted variant, including failures;
7. permit one small reversible TDD change only when a concrete evidence problem justifies it;
8. never auto-promote, auto-size, enable broker orders, or tune promotion gates;
9. roll the active challenger forward, reject it, or keep collecting according to preregistered criteria;
10. report the next falsifiable question rather than “AI confidence.”

The key is continuous learning, not continuous strategy mutation. Changing rules every week destroys the control group and converts adaptation into curve fitting.

## Promotion boundary

No strategy/model is eligible for broker-paper or live execution until it has:

- positive chronological walk-forward and untouched-forward expectancy after costs;
- profit factor above 1.2 with defined loss-side evidence;
- at least 100 independent forward observations across 20+ sessions;
- stable results across symbol, strategy, time, and regime slices;
- tolerable session-level drawdown under worse-but-plausible costs;
- tested order lifecycle, duplicate lock, reconciliation, aggregate exposure, and kill switch;
- explicit Neal approval.

Famous names do not get to waive these gates. Neither does a pretty model card.
